If you are looking to buy a business in Portugal, the first surprise is how few good ones are for sale. Most owners of small and medium-sized companies never advertise. They sell through personal contacts, or not at all. The deals worth buying are mostly off-market, which means you have to go and find them.

## What finding one good target actually involves

Behind a single serious conversation there is usually a long chain of work:

1. **Build a long list.** Business registers, sector codes, professional bodies and regulator lists. Each sector has its own sources, and none of them is clean.
2. **Size them.** Revenue and headcount are what tell you whether a target fits, but reliable financials cost money or take effort to proxy.
3. **Contact the owners.** In Portuguese, ideally, and with a reason for them to trust a stranger. Most will not reply.
4. **Screen for fit.** Client base, dependence on the owner, margins, price expectations. Many conversations end here.
5. **Only then** start talking about a deal.

None of it is hard. All of it takes time, and the time is spent before you have learned anything about a single business worth buying.

## Your options

**Do it yourself.** Cheapest in cash, most expensive in time. It works well if you have the language, the local credibility and a few weeks to dedicate. It works poorly if you are abroad, or if this is one of several things you are doing.

**Hire a broker on a mandate.** A broker works for you, runs a bespoke search and supports you through negotiation. This is the right choice for larger or more complex deals. It costs more, usually involves a retainer or success fee, and takes time to ramp up.

**Use a listings marketplace.** Fast to start, but what is listed tends to be what has already been shopped around. Useful as a reference point, less so as your only source.
